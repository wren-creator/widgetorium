Session 3: Certificates and Transport Security

Goal for this session: turn the three things you already noticed by hand in Session 1 into four documented findings, and add the one that needs a proper cipher probe. They are the kind auditors flag on real legacy deployments every week: a certificate that does not match its hostname, a certificate past its expiry, weak protocol and cipher support, and a missing HSTS header.

3.1 By Hand First

Before any TLS scanner runs, get the findings out of openssl and curl yourself. You did the first pass in Session 1.4; this is the full version.

openssl s_client -connect 127.0.0.1:8443 -servername widgetorium.local </dev/null 2>&1 \
  | openssl x509 -noout -subject -issuer -dates -ext subjectAltName
curl -ksv --tlsv1.0 --tls-max 1.0 https://127.0.0.1:8443/health.php   # does TLS 1.0 connect?
curl -ks  --tls-max 1.2 --ciphers AES128-SHA https://127.0.0.1:8443/health.php   # SHA1 CBC?
curl -ksI https://127.0.0.1:8443/ | grep -i strict-transport-security   # HSTS present?

You can name three of the four findings from that output alone: wrong name, self-signed, no HSTS. TLS 1.0 connecting and the SHA1 cipher being accepted are the fourth.

3.2 Then Let testssl.sh Do the Full Sweep

testssl.sh 127.0.0.1:8443

One run covers protocol versions, every cipher, DH size, expiry, and hostname mismatch in a readable report. sslyze is the alternative. Its job is coverage and a clean write-up, not to tell you something you could not already get from openssl s_client. Put its output next to your notes from 3.1 and confirm they agree.

3.3 Self-Signed, Wrong Name

The default certificate has CN=widget-store-prod-01, no subjectAltName at all, and is its own issuer. Nothing about it matches widgetorium.local or localhost.

openssl s_client -connect 127.0.0.1:8443 -servername widgetorium.local </dev/null 2>&1 \
  | openssl x509 -noout -subject -issuer -ext subjectAltName

testssl.sh reports this as a chain-of-trust failure and a hostname mismatch. The fix is a certificate from a trusted CA, or a managed internal CA for an internal-only app, with a SAN that actually lists the hostname. "It is just the CN" is a 2010 answer; modern clients ignore the CN and require the SAN.

3.4 Expired

Bring the lab up in the expired-certificate configuration and look again:

./start.sh --expired-cert
openssl s_client -connect 127.0.0.1:8443 </dev/null 2>/dev/null | openssl x509 -noout -dates

The validity window is entirely in January 2019. testssl.sh and every browser will refuse it outright. The lesson is not technical, it is operational: this is what "how did nobody notice" looks like from the outside. The fix is renewal automation and expiry monitoring, not a heroic manual replacement every two years.

3.5 Weak Protocols and Ciphers

The server offers TLS 1.0 and 1.1 alongside 1.2 and 1.3, accepts SHA1 CBC ciphers such as AES128-SHA, and serves 1024-bit Diffie-Hellman parameters.

curl -ks -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}\n' --tlsv1.0 --tls-max 1.0 https://127.0.0.1:8443/health.php
curl -ks -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}\n' --tls-max 1.2 --ciphers AES128-SHA https://127.0.0.1:8443/health.php

Both return 200. That is the finding.

A lab honesty note: the base image's OpenSSL is built without RC4, 3DES, and EXPORT ciphers, so you cannot demonstrate those specific suites here no matter what the config says. What Widgetorium does deliver, legacy protocol versions, SHA1 ciphers, weak DH, is the realistic profile of a system that has not been touched in a decade, and it is exactly what testssl.sh will light up in orange and red.

The fix: disable everything below TLS 1.2, cut the cipher list to modern AEAD suites, and use 2048-bit or stronger DH or, better, ECDHE. Running with WEAK_TLS=0 shows you the hardened baseline for comparison.

3.6 Missing HSTS

curl -ksI https://127.0.0.1:8443/ | grep -i strict-transport-security

Nothing comes back. Without Strict-Transport-Security, a client that first reaches the site over HTTP, or is pushed there by an attacker on the path, has no instruction to stay on HTTPS. Pair this with a strip demo in the browser dev tools to show what the missing header costs. The fix is one response header, sent over HTTPS, with a real max-age. Setting SEND_HSTS=1 adds it back.

3.7 What You Should Notice

Transport findings are the easiest category to scan for and the easiest to wave away, "it is only the cert," "nobody uses TLS 1.0 anymore." Reading the handshake yourself with openssl s_client, before the report tidies it into a colour, is what makes you stop waving. Each line is a specific promise the server is failing to keep: this connection is with who you think, it has not been tampered with, it cannot be quietly downgraded. A trainee who can walk an auditor's client through that report, line by line, is worth more than one who can only forward the PDF.
